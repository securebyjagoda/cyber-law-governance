# 201 CMR 17.00

## Overview

201 CMR 17.00, *Standards for the Protection of Personal Information of Residents of the Commonwealth*, is the primary Massachusetts regulation governing how organizations must protect personal information. It establishes minimum administrative, technical, and physical safeguards for organizations that own, license, store, maintain, collect, or process personal information belonging to Massachusetts residents.

For organizations conducting background checks, contractor screening, vendor due diligence, or investor reviews, 201 CMR 17.00 serves as the foundational cybersecurity regulation governing how collected information must be protected.

---

## Why It Matters

Due diligence processes routinely collect sensitive personal information, including Social Security Numbers, financial information, addresses, and identity documents. These activities frequently fall within the scope of 201 CMR 17.00.

Unlike many privacy laws that focus on transparency or disclosure obligations, 201 CMR 17.00 focuses heavily on cybersecurity and information security requirements. It establishes baseline security controls that apply regardless of where the organization is located if Massachusetts resident data is involved.

For this reason, it serves as the backbone law governing the protection of due diligence information throughout its lifecycle.

---

## Scope

The regulation applies to any individual, organization, business, or entity that owns, licenses, stores, maintains, collects, or processes personal information relating to a Massachusetts resident.

Jurisdiction is based on the residency of the individual whose information is being processed, rather than the physical location of the organization handling the information.

---

## Protected Information

201 CMR 17.00 defines personal information as a Massachusetts resident's first name or first initial and last name combined with any of the following:

- Social Security Number
- Driver's License Number
- State Identification Card Number
- Financial Account Number
- Credit Card Number
- Debit Card Number
- Information permitting access to a financial account

The regulation therefore applies to many data elements commonly collected during third-party screening and due diligence processes.

---

## Key Requirements

The centerpiece of 201 CMR 17.00 is the requirement that organizations develop, implement, maintain, and regularly review a Written Information Security Program (WISP).

A compliant WISP must:

- Designate an individual responsible for implementation and oversight.
- Identify records, systems, devices, and storage locations containing personal information.
- Assess internal and external risks to personal information.
- Evaluate existing safeguards and their effectiveness.
- Restrict access based on legitimate business need.
- Include employee security awareness training.
- Define incident documentation and response procedures.
- Establish monitoring procedures.
- Include oversight of third-party service providers.

The regulation also requires secure authentication mechanisms, encryption, access controls, monitoring capabilities, firewall protection, system patching, malware protection, and periodic review of security safeguards.

---

## Relationship to Due Diligence

201 CMR 17.00 applies across nearly every phase of the due diligence process.

During verification activities, organizations may collect personal information such as addresses, government-issued identifiers, and ownership information.

During financial reviews, organizations may collect financial account information and credit-related information.

During monitoring and retention activities, organizations must continue protecting previously collected information through appropriate security controls and retention practices.

Although 201 CMR 17.00 does not determine whether information may be collected, it governs how collected information must be secured throughout its lifecycle.

---

## Relationship to Other Laws

201 CMR 17.00 operates alongside many other state and federal laws relevant to due diligence.

Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 93H establishes breach notification obligations when protected information is exposed. 201 CMR 17.00 provides the security controls intended to prevent those breaches from occurring.

The Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) introduces privacy and security requirements for financial information. Organizations frequently satisfy portions of GLBA's Safeguards Rule by implementing controls already required under 201 CMR 17.00.

The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) governs the use and handling of consumer reports during screening activities. 201 CMR 17.00 supplies the technical safeguards protecting that information.

For this reason, 201 CMR 17.00 functions as the foundational cybersecurity framework underlying many due diligence-related legal obligations.

---

## Governance Implications

Organizations handling due diligence information should maintain:

- Written Information Security Program (WISP)
- Data Classification Policy
- Data Retention Schedule
- Vendor Management Program
- Third-Party Risk Assessment Process
- Incident Response Plan
- Security Awareness Training Program

Together, these governance mechanisms support compliance with the regulation's administrative requirements.

---

## Cybersecurity Implications

Compliance with 201 CMR 17.00 requires the implementation of both organizational and technical safeguards.

Examples include:

- Encryption
- Access Control
- Authentication Controls
- Audit Logging
- Security Monitoring
- Secure Data Disposal
- Vulnerability Management
- Patch Management
- Malware Protection

These controls collectively support confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal information.
