# Financial Information

## Overview

Financial Information consists of data used to evaluate an organization's financial health, stability, creditworthiness, and ability to satisfy contractual obligations.

Organizations rely on financial information to assess financial risk, business viability, fraud exposure, and long-term operational sustainability.

---

## Common Examples

- Audited Financial Statements
- Balance Sheets
- Revenue, Profit, and Margin Information
- Credit Reports
- Bankruptcy Records
- Financial Liabilities
- Funding History
- Cash Flow Reports
- Fraud Indicators

---

## Sensitivity Classification

**Sensitivity Level:** High

Financial information often contains confidential business information and, in some cases, nonpublic personal financial information protected by federal and state regulations.

Improper handling may result in financial loss, fraud, competitive harm, regulatory enforcement, and unauthorized disclosure of sensitive information.

---

## Why It Matters

Organizations use this information to:

- Evaluate financial stability
- Assess creditworthiness
- Identify fraud indicators
- Determine contractual and investment risk
- Support risk-based decision-making

---

## Special Considerations

Financial information may be subject to additional regulatory requirements when it contains consumer financial information or originates from consumer reporting agencies.

Organizations should ensure:

- Information is collected for a legitimate business purpose
- Access is limited on a need-to-know basis
- Financial records remain accurate and current
- Retention periods are documented
- Disclosure is appropriately controlled

---

## Recommended Governance Approach

- Maintain a Written Information Security Program (WISP)
- Establish a Data Retention Schedule
- Conduct Vendor Risk Assessments
- Maintain Records of Processing Activities (ROPA)
- Document business justification for collection and use
